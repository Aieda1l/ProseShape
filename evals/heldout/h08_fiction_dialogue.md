Marcus found his brother on the back porch, staring out at the dark orchard. The air smelled of rain and overripe apples. Crickets sang in the tall grass.

"You're really selling it," Marcus said quietly.

Daniel didn't turn around. "The bank isn't giving us a choice. Dad left us more debt than trees."

Marcus felt a lump form in his throat. "This orchard was his whole life. It's where we grew up. It's not just land, Danny. It's who we are."

"I know." Daniel's voice cracked. "But holding on to the past won't pay the mortgage. Sometimes letting go is the only way to honor what someone built."

Marcus sat down beside him on the worn steps. For a long time, neither of them spoke. Somewhere in the orchard, an apple dropped to the ground.

"Okay," Marcus finally said. "But I'm keeping his pruning shears."

Daniel laughed, a small, broken sound, and in that moment, Marcus knew they would be all right.
