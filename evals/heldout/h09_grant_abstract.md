Food insecurity remains a pressing and multifaceted challenge affecting millions of American households. This proposed study aims to delve into the complex interplay between community food pantries and health outcomes among older adults. Building on preliminary data from 412 pantry clients in three Ohio counties, which revealed that 61% of respondents aged 65 and older reported skipping medications to afford food, we will conduct a 24-month longitudinal cohort study (target n = 900). Specifically, we will examine whether monthly produce boxes are associated with changes in HbA1c, blood pressure, and self-reported well-being. We hypothesize that participants receiving produce boxes will show modest improvements in glycemic control relative to matched controls. Ultimately, this research has the potential to inform pivotal policy decisions and foster more equitable, resilient, and health-promoting food systems for vulnerable populations.
