My father taught me to drive in the parking lot of a church that had closed the year before. He said it was the only flat, empty place in town where nobody would mind if I hit the curb, and I hit it more than once.

He was not a patient man in most things, but he was patient that summer. He never grabbed the wheel. When I stalled, which was often, he would wait for the engine to go quiet and then say, "Again," as if we had all the time in the world. Maybe we did, then.

The last lesson was in August. I drove the two miles home on real streets while he sat with his hands folded in his lap, looking out the window and pretending not to watch the speedometer. I parked in front of the house, badly, and he got out without a word and went inside.

I've been driving for thirty years now. I still check the mirrors the way he showed me, left, rearview, right, before I pull away from a curb. Some lessons don't need to be repeated to stay with you.
