The *Halcyon* drifted in the silence of the outer belt, its hull ticking as it cooled. Commander Rhee floated before the viewport, watching the derelict probe tumble slowly against the stars.

"It's been out here for two hundred years," said Ito-Marsh, the ship's engineer, her voice barely above a whisper. "Two hundred years, and it's still transmitting."

Rhee felt a chill run down her spine. Her heart pounded in her chest. The signal was faint but unmistakable: the same three tones, repeating every eleven seconds.

"We have to bring it aboard," Ito-Marsh said. "Think about what we could learn."

"Or we leave it," Rhee replied. "Some things aren't meant to be found. Curiosity isn't just a gift — it's a responsibility."

The engineer was silent for a long moment. Then she nodded slowly. "You're right. It's not our story to finish."

Rhee turned back to the viewport. The probe's antenna caught the light of the distant sun, a single silver thread against the dark. And in that moment, she understood that the universe wasn't something to be conquered, but something to be witnessed.

She marked the coordinates in the log and set a course for home.
