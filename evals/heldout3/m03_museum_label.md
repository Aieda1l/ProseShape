Harbor at Dusk, 1958
Oil on canvas
Agnes Thorvaldsen (American, born Norway, 1911–1987)
Gift of the Wendell Family, 1994

Agnes Thorvaldsen's *Harbor at Dusk* stands as a powerful testament to the artist's enduring fascination with light, water, and memory. Painted during her final summer in Gloucester, Massachusetts, the work captures the fleeting moment when daylight surrenders to evening, transforming the working harbor into a symphony of violet and amber.

Thorvaldsen, who emigrated from Bergen at the age of nine, often spoke of the harbors of her childhood. Here, those memories seem to merge seamlessly with the New England present. The loose, gestural brushwork reflects her growing interest in Abstract Expressionism, while the recognizable masts and pier anchor the composition in observed reality.

Notably, Thorvaldsen applied the paint for the water with a palette knife, building up ridges that catch the gallery light and appear to move as viewers walk past. This painting was first exhibited at the Rockport Art Association in 1959, where it received the association's Gold Medal.

Ultimately, *Harbor at Dusk* invites us to reflect on the delicate interplay between place and identity — a reminder that home is not merely a location, but a feeling we carry with us.
