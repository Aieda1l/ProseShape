The hospital parking garage was nearly empty when Iris finally found her car. She sat behind the wheel without starting the engine, the keys heavy in her hand.

Her mother's voice echoed in her mind. You never visit. You never call. As if Iris hadn't driven four hours every other weekend for two years.

Her chest tightened. Her throat burned. She pressed her palms against her eyes until colors bloomed in the dark.

Her phone buzzed. It was her brother, Cal.

"How is she?" he asked.

"The same," Iris said. "She asked for you."

A pause. "I can't get away until Friday."

"You never can." The words came out sharper than she meant. "It's not just about showing up, Cal. It's about being someone she can count on."

He was quiet for a long moment. "I know," he said softly. "I'm trying."

She looked out at the concrete pillars, the flickering fluorescent lights, the oil stains shaped like countries she would never visit. And in that moment, she realized that she had been carrying this alone for far too long — and that maybe, just maybe, she didn't have to anymore.

"Come Friday," she said. "I'll wait."
