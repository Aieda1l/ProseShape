New Study Suggests Afternoon Naps May Boost Memory in Older Adults

A groundbreaking new study from researchers at the University of Halden has shed fascinating light on the relationship between napping and memory. The findings, published in the Journal of Sleep and Cognition, suggest that a short afternoon nap may be associated with better memory performance in adults over 60.

The research team followed 1,240 participants for three years, tracking their sleep habits and testing their memory every six months. Participants who regularly napped for 20 to 40 minutes scored on average 8% higher on recall tests than those who did not nap. Notably, longer naps of more than 90 minutes were linked to slightly lower scores.

"We were surprised by how consistent the pattern was across different groups," said lead author Dr. Elin Varga. "But this is an observational study, so we can't say that napping causes the improvement."

Experts not involved in the research urged caution. Dr. Kwame Asante, a sleep researcher at Westbrook Medical Center, noted that people who nap may differ in other ways, such as having more flexible schedules or better overall health.

Ultimately, this study serves as a powerful reminder that sleep plays a vital role in healthy aging. As researchers continue to unravel the mysteries of the brain, one thing is clear: the humble nap deserves a second look.
