Review: *The Salt Archive* by Imogen Hale

Imogen Hale's debut novel, *The Salt Archive*, is a sweeping, ambitious tapestry of memory, loss, and the stories families tell themselves. Set in a fading coastal town in Maine, it follows archivist Ada Calder as she uncovers a box of letters that rewrites her grandmother's past.

There is much to admire here. Hale's prose is often luminous, and her descriptions of the sea are a testament to her keen eye for detail. One line in particular stayed with me: "The tide took what it wanted and returned only what it could not use." The first half moves with a quiet, confident rhythm, and Ada is a compelling, layered protagonist.

However, the novel is not without its flaws. The second half leans too heavily on coincidence, and a late twist involving Ada's brother feels unearned. At 480 pages, the book is also simply too long; I found myself skimming the middle chapters.

Ultimately, *The Salt Archive* is a flawed but rewarding read that showcases a talented new voice. Readers who appreciate atmospheric, character-driven fiction will find much to love, even if the journey occasionally loses its way.

Rating: 3.5 out of 5
