In today's rapidly evolving educational landscape, the role of homework remains a topic of intense debate. Okafor and Lindqvist (2019) synthesized 41 studies published between 1995 and 2017 and found a modest positive association between homework and achievement (r = .19), with the association notably stronger in secondary than in primary grades. Importantly, the benefits appear to plateau: Brandt et al. (2021) reported no additional gains in mathematics scores for students who spent more than 70 minutes per night on homework. As Mehta (2020) puts it, "the question is no longer whether to assign homework, but how much is too much" (p. 112). These findings underscore the critical need for educators to carefully consider not just the quantity of homework, but its quality and purpose.
