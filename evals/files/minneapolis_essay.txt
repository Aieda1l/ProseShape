Moving to Minneapolis in January was, in hindsight, a bold choice. I remember stepping off the plane and feeling the cold hit me like a wall — a sharp, biting reminder that I was no longer in Phoenix. My breath caught in my throat as the wind whipped across the parking lot.

Those first few weeks were a whirlwind of new experiences. I learned to layer, to scrape ice off my windshield, and to embrace the quiet beauty of a snow-covered morning. The city, with its frozen lakes and cozy coffee shops, slowly began to feel less like a strange place and more like home.

Looking back, I realize that the cold taught me something important: that discomfort is often the doorway to growth. It wasn't just about surviving the winter — it was about discovering a version of myself I never knew existed.
