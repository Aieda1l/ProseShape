Rosa set her gym bag down by the door. "Dad, I need to tell you something. I'm quitting the swim team."

Her father looked up from his laptop, surprise flickering across his face. "Quitting? But you've been swimming since you were seven. Don't you think you'll regret giving up on something you've worked so hard for?"

"I've thought about it a lot," Rosa said, her voice trembling slightly. "I think sometimes the bravest thing you can do is let go of something that no longer serves you. I've been swimming for you, not for me."

Her father was silent for a long moment, the weight of her words settling over him. "I suppose I always wanted you to have the discipline I never had," he admitted. "But discipline without joy is just another kind of prison."

Rosa felt tears prick her eyes. "So you're not mad?"

He closed his laptop and smiled gently. "I'm not mad. I'm proud of you for knowing yourself. That takes more courage than any race."

She hugged him, and in that moment, the distance between them seemed to disappear.
