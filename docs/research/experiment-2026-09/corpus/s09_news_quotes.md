In a move that marks a pivotal moment for the region's transit future, the Harbor County Board of Supervisors voted 4-1 on Tuesday to approve a $48 million expansion of the Route 9 bus line, adding 11 new stops and extending service hours to midnight.

"This is about giving people a real alternative to driving," said Supervisor Lena Kowalczyk, who sponsored the measure. "For a lot of families in the east county, the bus is the only way to get to work."

Supervisor Mark Deaver, who cast the lone dissenting vote, argued that the plan relies too heavily on state matching funds. "If Sacramento pulls back, we're left holding the bill," he said.

According to a 2024 county survey, 38% of east county households do not own a car. Ridership on Route 9 has grown 22% since 2022, reaching roughly 6,400 weekday boardings.

Construction is expected to begin in March, with the first new stops opening by fall 2026. The expansion underscores the county's evolving commitment to equitable, sustainable, and accessible transportation for all residents.
